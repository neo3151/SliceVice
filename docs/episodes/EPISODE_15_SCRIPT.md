# EPISODE 15 SCRIPT: "PALM SPRINGS MILE"
**Date:** Feb 28, 1980
**Historical Anchor:** Hialeah, Florida. The working-class Cuban stronghold. Hialeah Park Race Track — the "Grande Dame of Racing" — listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1979. Its resident flock of flamingos is the only real thing in a city built on sweat, sewing machines, and concrete block.

---

**Panel 01**
AXEL: (Caption) Day fifty-nine. Elio's cousin came through. The donor transmission is from a '77 Grand Prix that lost a fight with a telephone pole on Card Sound Road. The bell housing mates. The gear ratios are close enough. Elio says the car will be ready tomorrow.
AXEL: (Caption) In the meantime, he's lent me a Datsun B210 the color of a bruise. It has AM radio, no air conditioning, and a rearview mirror held on with electrical tape. It's the ugliest car I've ever driven. I love it because it's not mine and nothing depends on it.

**Panel 02**
AXEL: (Caption) Hialeah. I'm here for a throttle body. A '79 Pontiac unit that Elio says will give the LeMans an extra thirty horses if I can find one that isn't corroded. He told me to try Hank Dockery's yard on East 8th Avenue—"the only Anglo junkyard left in Hialeah."
AXEL: (Caption) Palm Springs Mile. W 49th Street. The commercial strip. Cuban bakeries, auto-parts stores, furniture outlets. A botanica with plaster saints in the window and the smell of sandalwood rolling out the open door like fog.

**Panel 03**
AXEL: (Caption) The bakeries are already fogged at seven AM. The line for pan cubano is out the door. Women in house dresses. Men in work boots. Kids in Catholic school uniforms eating croquetas out of paper bags on the sidewalk. This is a neighborhood that gets up before the sun because the factories don't wait.
AXEL: (Caption) Every third house has a rooster in the yard. They are not decorative.

**Panel 04**
AXEL: (Caption) Dockery's yard. Chain-link fence, razor wire, a hand-painted sign that reads DOCKERY SALVAGE - CASH ONLY. Hank Dockery is seventy, sun-cooked, wearing a mesh-back cap and coveralls that were blue twenty years ago. He looks like the last man standing from a Florida that existed before air conditioning.
HANK: (Dialogue) "Pontiac throttle body. Seventy-nine. Yeah, I got one—maybe. Row 14, past the Chryslers. If the iguanas haven't eaten the gasket, it's yours for forty."

**Panel 05**
AXEL: (Caption) Row 14. The cars are stacked three deep—Impalas, Novas, a Dodge Dart with a palm tree growing through the engine bay. The heat is suffocating. The metal radiates it back at you from every surface. I find the Pontiac wedged between a rusted El Camino and a Buick Electra that looks like it was parked here during the Kennedy administration.
AXEL: (Caption) The throttle body is intact. The gasket is shot, but Elio can cut a new one. I pull it with a socket set that Hank lent me, my hands burning on every surface.

**Panel 06**
HANK: (Dialogue) "You notice anything about the yard lately?"
AXEL: (Dialogue) "It's hot."
HANK: (Dialogue) "Besides that. I'm down thirty cars this month. The city's buying up scrap metal like they're building an ark. Metrorail expansion. They're buying steel from every yard south of Opa-locka."
AXEL: (Dialogue) "That's progress."
HANK: (Dialogue) "That's somebody at the county who knows something we don't. You don't build infrastructure that fast unless you're expecting a lot of new people."

**Panel 07**
AXEL: (Caption) I don't follow up on Hank's theory. I pay for the throttle body, put it in the Datsun's trunk, and drive south on East 4th Avenue toward the track. I don't know why I'm going to the track. I don't bet on horses. I don't even like horses. But Elio said the restoration is almost done, and something about a building that beautiful in a city this hard feels like a contradiction I need to see.

**Panel 08**
AXEL: (Caption) Hialeah Park. The Grande Dame. The grandstand is white Art Deco—tiered columns, clean lines, the elegance of something designed before the world learned to cut corners. The infield lake is still and dark. And the flamingos.
AXEL: (Caption) The flamingos stand in the shallow water like they're waiting for instructions that were cancelled forty years ago. Pink. Absurdly, impossibly pink. One leg up. Necks curved into question marks. They don't move. They don't need to. They've decided this is where they live, and nothing—not the garment factories, not the roosters, not the concrete—has convinced them otherwise.

**Panel 09**
AXEL: (Caption) I lean on the terrace railing. The track is empty. The last race was hours ago. The only sound is the wind across the infield water and the distant hum of the Palmetto Expressway.
AXEL: (Caption) The horses here run in circles. Start line is the finish line. They run as hard as they can and end up exactly where they began. The crowd cheers because the order of arrival matters, even when the destination doesn't.
AXEL: (Caption) At least they know where the finish line is.

**Panel 10**
AXEL: (Caption) On the way back to Little Havana, I pass a botanica on Palm Springs Mile. BOTANICA SIETE POTENCIAS. Seven Powers. The door is open. The smell of sandalwood and copal resin stops me on the sidewalk like a hand on my chest.
AXEL: (Caption) I go in. I don't know why. The shelves are lined with plaster saints, glass-jar candles, bundles of dried herbs, and small bottles of oils with handwritten labels. Elegguá. Changó. Yemayá. Names I don't know attached to forces I can feel in the thin, heavy air.

**Panel 11**
YOLANDA: (Dialogue) "Can I help you?"
AXEL: (Caption) The woman behind the counter is maybe forty. Dark hair pulled back. A thin gold chain with a pendant I don't recognize. She doesn't look mystical. She looks like someone who works retail and knows what she's selling.
AXEL: (Dialogue) "I want to buy a candle."
YOLANDA: (Dialogue) "Who's it for?"
AXEL: (Dialogue) "I don't know yet."
YOLANDA: (Caption) She looks at me for a long moment. Then she takes a white candle from the shelf, lights it with a kitchen match, and says something in Yoruba—soft, rhythmic, private. She sets it on the counter between us.
YOLANDA: (Dialogue) "You don't need to know who it's for. The flame knows."

**Panel 12**
AXEL: (Caption) I pay for the candle. Three dollars. She wraps the base in aluminum foil so the wax won't drip in the car. I walk out into the Hialeah afternoon—95 degrees, zero breeze, the sidewalk so hot it softens the soles of my sneakers—carrying a lit candle in one hand and a Pontiac throttle body in the other.
AXEL: (Caption) The Datsun's AM radio picks up WIOD. Between a news segment about the grain embargo and a weather report (high of 91, humidity 85%, chance of afternoon thunderstorms: zero), Queen's "Crazy Little Thing Called Love" comes on.
AXEL: (Caption) I turn it up.

**Panel 13**
AXEL: (Caption) That's the first thing I've done in fifty-nine days purely because I wanted to.

**Panel 14**
AXEL: (Caption) I drive south through Hialeah with the windows down and a candle burning in the cupholder. The smoke bends west in the slipstream. The flamingos don't move. The roosters don't stop. The garment factories hum behind their concrete walls. And Queen tells me it's a crazy little thing.
AXEL: (Caption) It is.
