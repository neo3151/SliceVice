# EPISODE 16 SCRIPT: "THE MESH"
**Date:** March 5, 1980
**Historical Anchor:** Little Haiti (Lemon City), Miami. NE 2nd Avenue corridor. Established by Haitian refugees fleeing the Duvalier dictatorship, built on faith, analog ingenuity, and the deliberate art of being invisible to the systems that refuse to see you.

---

**Panel 01**
AXEL: (Caption) Day sixty-five. The LeMans is back. Elio rebuilt her transmission with the Grand Prix donor, cut a new throttle body gasket from a cereal box and gasket sealant he mixed himself, and tuned the idle until she purrs like a lie told with confidence. Third gear is a revelation. Fourth is a promise.
AXEL: (Caption) He charged me six hundred dollars and a favor owed. The favor comes today.

**Panel 02**
REYA: (Dialogue, over radio) "Simple delivery. Sealed envelope. Take it to NE 54th Street, Little Haiti. A man named Jean-Pierre will be waiting at the Notre Dame d'Haiti church. Give him the envelope. Don't open it. Don't ask."
AXEL: (Dialogue) "Courier run."
REYA: (Dialogue) "Courier run. The kind you used to do before everything got complicated."
AXEL: (Dialogue) "Everything was always complicated. I just didn't know it yet."

**Panel 03**
AXEL: (Caption) I drive north on I-95 and exit at 54th Street. The transition is instantaneous. The highway off-ramp deposits you into a different country. The signage switches from English to Creole. The storefronts are hand-painted in blues and yellows that would look festive if the buildings didn't look like they'd been standing since the Hoover administration. The sidewalks are cracked and lined with crates of produce—plantains, yams, scotch bonnet peppers in plastic bags.
AXEL: (Caption) The Vanguard's thermal trackers don't work here. I noticed it three blocks in. The dashboard Scanner—the one that picks up Compliance Drone signals—went silent. Not jammed. Just... nothing. Like the frequency doesn't exist in this airspace.

**Panel 04**
AXEL: (Caption) I park the LeMans under a Royal Poinciana tree on NE 2nd Avenue. Orange blossoms fall on the hood like a benediction I didn't ask for. A woman in a white headwrap walks past carrying a basin on her hip. She doesn't look at the car. Nobody looks at the car. In Little Haiti, not looking is a form of protection—for you and for the thing you're not looking at.

**Panel 05**
AXEL: (Caption) Notre Dame d'Haiti. A modest church—concrete block, painted white, a wooden cross over the entrance. The door is open. Inside: wooden pews, votive candles, the smell of incense and floor wax. A mural of the Virgin Mary on the back wall, painted in the Haitian style—vivid blues and golds, her face dark-skinned and impossibly serene.
AXEL: (Caption) A man is sitting in the last pew, soldering something that looks like a radio antenna coiled on his lap.

**Panel 06**
JEAN-PIERRE: (Dialogue) "You're Reya's courier."
AXEL: (Dialogue) "I'm Reya's courier."
JEAN-PIERRE: (Dialogue) "Sit. The envelope can wait two minutes. You look like a man who could use a church."
AXEL: (Caption) He's mid-forties, lean, wire-rimmed glasses that sit crookedly on his nose. His hands are precise—the hands of someone who works with frequencies measured in fractions. He has a short-wave radio antenna coiled over his shoulder like a pet snake. His voice is calm, accented, patient. He speaks like a man who has learned that rushing gets you deported.

**Panel 07**
AXEL: (Dialogue) "What's the antenna for?"
JEAN-PIERRE: (Dialogue) "The Mesh. The Americans built a grid to watch us. So we built a grid they can't see."
AXEL: (Dialogue) "The signal went dead when I crossed into the neighborhood."
JEAN-PIERRE: (Dialogue) "Not dead. Redirected. The Mesh doesn't block their signal—it bends it. Like light through water. They think they're scanning the neighborhood. They're scanning a reflection."
AXEL: (Caption) I hand him the envelope. He takes it without looking at it, slides it into his jacket, and produces two bottles of Prestige beer from under the pew. He opens them both with a lighter.

**Panel 08**
JEAN-PIERRE: (Dialogue) "An immigrant chooses to leave. A refugee is chosen. When we got here, nobody gave us credit. The Cubans got asylum. We got called 'economic migrants' and were targeted for Vanguard foreclosure."
JEAN-PIERRE: (Dialogue) "So we built our own system. The Mesh. It isn't just radio frequencies—the scrap copper wire physically insulates the neighborhood blocks from Vanguard's Soul Extractors. No corporate overhead. No souls defaulted. Just analog relays hidden in palm trees, maintaining our physical gravity so we don't get erased."

**Panel 09**
AXEL: (Caption) We sit on the church steps and drink beer as the sun drops behind the rooflines. The air smells like charcoal grills and frying plantain from the grocery two doors down. A radio inside the store plays Tabou Combo—Haitian compas. Brass and guitar pouring onto the sidewalk like something joyful that escaped from a locked room. I don't recognize the song, but the rhythm gets into my chest and stays there.
AXEL: (Caption) Kids are playing soccer in the street with a ball that's half-deflated. They're laughing. The ball bounces off the curb and through the LeMans' open passenger window. I pick it up and throw it back. A kid waves. No words. No drama. Just a ball and a wave.

**Panel 10**
JEAN-PIERRE: (Dialogue) "The Coast Guard channels have been busy. Busier than I've ever heard them."
AXEL: (Dialogue) "Busy how?"
JEAN-PIERRE: (Dialogue) "Encrypted traffic. Boat tracking. We can't read the content, but we can count the transmissions. The volume has tripled in the last two weeks. Something is moving in the Straits. A lot of somethings."
AXEL: (Dialogue) "Drugs?"
JEAN-PIERRE: (Dialogue) "Drugs don't need that many boats. And they don't encrypt Coast Guard channels for drugs—they just chase them. This is different. This is the government watching something they don't want to stop. They're watching it come."

**Panel 11**
AXEL: (Caption) I file it away. Not my problem. Not yet. The beer is cold, the compas is warm, and for fifteen minutes on a church stoop in Little Haiti, I'm not a courier, not a fugitive, not the man who punched the Chief Compliance Officer of the Western Hemisphere in the jaw. I'm just a guy with a beer, watching kids play soccer in a neighborhood the city pretends doesn't exist.
AXEL: (Caption) Jean-Pierre doesn't ask my name. In Little Haiti, anonymity isn't rudeness. It's a gift you give someone without being asked.

**Panel 12**
AXEL: (Caption) I drive south on NE 2nd Avenue as the streetlights—what few there are—flicker on. The Mesh-Network's invisible grid hums above me in the palm trees and steeples, carrying signals I can't hear to people I'll never meet.
AXEL: (Caption) In the rearview mirror, the church steeple shrinks against the dusk sky. The antenna is visible if you know what to look for—a crude short-wave array lashed to the bell tower with wire. A middle finger aimed at every satellite in orbit.
AXEL: (Caption) I liked Jean-Pierre. That's dangerous. Liking people in this city is an unfunded liability.
