# EPISODE 18 SCRIPT: "THE INFIELD"
**Date:** March 15, 1980 — Night
**Historical Anchor:** Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park, Key Biscayne. The Cape Florida Lighthouse — the oldest standing structure in Miami-Dade County, built 1825. Relit in 1978 after a century of darkness. A beacon that was turned off and turned back on. The metaphor doesn't require explanation.

---

**Panel 01**
AXEL: (Caption) Day seventy-five. The Rickenbacker Causeway at night.
AXEL: (Caption) One road. Two miles of concrete over black water. The toll booth at the Miami end is a fluorescent island in the dark—a woman in a booth reading a paperback, waiting for your quarters.
AXEL: (Caption) I have exact change. For the first time in seventy-five days, I hit the basket without fumbling. The gate lifts. The causeway opens. The LeMans' headlights cut a tunnel through the salt air.

**Panel 02**
AXEL: (Caption) Key Biscayne. The anti-Miami. The streets are dark by design. No neon. No strip malls. No storefronts with bars on the windows. Just hedgerows, Spanish tile roofs, and the sound of the Atlantic doing what it's been doing since before the limestone formed.
AXEL: (Caption) Nixon kept a house here. The "Winter White House." He came over fifty times during his presidency, sat on Bay Lane, and managed Vietnam from a compound with a helicopter pad in the bay. Even the President needed a place where the phone didn't ring.

**Panel 03**
AXEL: (Caption) I drive past Crandon Park, past the golf course, past the turnoff to the Sonesta Beach Hotel where corporate retreats happen behind tinted glass. I drive to the end of the road. The very end. Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park. The gate is closed. I park the LeMans on the shoulder and walk.
AXEL: (Caption) The lighthouse is ahead. A white conical tower against a sky that's more stars than sky. The beam sweeps overhead every thirty seconds—a slow, mechanical rotation. Light. Dark. Light. Dark. The rhythm of something that was designed to keep people from crashing.

**Panel 04**
AXEL: (Caption) I sit on the seawall. The Atlantic is ten feet below me—black, moving, alive with the sound of water organizing itself against rock. The wind smells like salt and the particular kind of nothing that exists only on the edge of a continent at one in the morning.
AXEL: (Caption) I reach into my jacket pocket. The tape reel. A spool the size of my fist. Twenty minutes of magnetic oxide on Mylar—the only recording of my mother's voice, extracted from the Vanguard's servers at the Freedom Tower, sitting in my pocket for twenty-three days.

**Panel 05**
AXEL: (Caption) I've been carrying it since the reformat. Twenty-three days. I haven't played it. I told myself I was waiting for the right moment. The truth is simpler: I'm afraid that what's on the tape will be exactly what I remember, and I'll have to feel everything I've been driving away from.
AXEL: (Caption) The lighthouse beam sweeps over me. Light. Dark.

**Panel 06**
AXEL: (Caption) I brought a portable player. A Panasonic RQ-309—micro-cassette, adapted with a reel-to-reel spindle mount that Elio fabricated from a turntable motor. It runs on four C-batteries and sounds like it's being played through a tin can on a string. But it plays.
AXEL: (Caption) I thread the tape. The oxide is thin—delicate. My hands are shaking and I don't know if it's the cold or the three cafecitos or the fact that I'm about to hear a ghost.

**Panel 07**
AXEL: (Caption) I press play.
AXEL: (Caption) Static. The hiss of old tape. Then—

**Panel 08**
AXEL'S MOTHER: (Dialogue, on tape) "Axel. It's late. You should be sleeping. But I wanted to tell you a story before—before tomorrow. This is the story of the fish and the moon."
AXEL: (Caption) Her voice. It's her voice. Not the synthesized version the Vanguard played through their PA drones. Not the weaponized version that Vance modulated to throw me off the causeway in Episode 8. The real one. Warm. Tired. The voice of a woman who knows she is being recorded and is trying to sound calm for a seven-year-old who doesn't know that tomorrow will be the last morning.

**Panel 09**
AXEL'S MOTHER: (Dialogue, on tape) "The fish lived in a deep, dark part of the ocean where the sunlight never reached. He could only see the world when the moon came out and its light came down through the water. And one night, the fish swam up to the surface and said to the moon, 'Why do you only come out at night?'"
AXEL'S MOTHER: (Dialogue, on tape) "And the moon said, 'Because the night is when the lost things need the most light.'"

**Panel 10**
AXEL: (Caption) I was seven. She recorded this in the kitchen of our apartment in Coral Terrace—I can hear the refrigerator humming in the background, the specific rattle of the compressor that I forgot I remembered. She recorded it the night before they took her. She must have known. She made a bedtime story sound like a goodbye, and I didn't understand until right now, on a seawall in Key Biscayne, twenty-three years too late.
AXEL: (Caption) The tape runs out. The player clicks. The lighthouse beam sweeps. Light. Dark. Light.

**Panel 11**
AXEL: (Caption) I rewind the tape. I play it again.
AXEL: (Caption) I play it again.
AXEL: (Caption) The ocean doesn't care. The lighthouse doesn't care. The stars don't rearrange themselves to acknowledge that a man on a seawall just heard his mother's voice for the first time in eleven years. But the sound of her—the specific frequency of her, the warmth and the tiredness and the love that's doing its best to sound like a bedtime story when it's really doing the work of a will—that sound enters me through the small speaker of a jury-rigged Panasonic and rewrites something in my chest that I didn't know was still running code.

**Panel 12**
AXEL: (Caption) I put the tape back in my jacket pocket. I sit in the LeMans with the engine off and the windows down and listen to the ocean.
AXEL: (Caption) Somewhere out there—past the reef, past the Gulf Stream, past the continental shelf—the Florida Straits connect Miami to Cuba in ninety miles of warm, dark water. And in that water, tonight, I can hear engines. Faint. Distant. Not one—several. Boat engines, moving north. Toward Miami.
AXEL: (Caption) I can't see them. The water is black. But the sound carries. One engine. Then another. Then a third. Then silence. Then another.

AXEL: (Caption) Something is coming.

AXEL: (Caption) Dawn comes slowly. The sky turns from black to the deep blue of a bruised plum, then to gray, then to the pale pink of a city that wakes up every morning and pretends it wasn't built on a swamp and a prayer. The lighthouse beam fades into the daylight, still spinning, still sweeping, unnecessary now but unwilling to stop.
AXEL: (Caption) I start the engine. The LeMans turns over on the first try. The rebuilt transmission engages. I drive north, across the causeway, back toward the mainland, back toward the Grid.
AXEL: (Caption) The night is when the lost things need the most light.
