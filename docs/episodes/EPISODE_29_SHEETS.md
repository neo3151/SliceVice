# EPISODE 29 PANEL BREAKDOWN: "THE MESH ROUTE" (9 PANELS)
**Date:** May 17–18, 1980 — 10:30 PM to 1 AM
**Tone:** Driving blind through a city that knows itself better than you do. Trust transferred through geography. The trunk carrying something irreplaceable.
**Color Key:** No headlights for most of the episode — the city in darkness lit only by distant fire glow (orange, low on the horizon to the west), porch lights off, the occasional streetlamp. Jean-Pierre's map: pencil on white paper, architectural handwriting. The wooden box: plain, unfinished wood, slightly warm-looking. Elio's garage: work-light amber, familiar.

---

**Page 1 (The Checkpoint / The Map)**
- **Panel 01:** (Interior/Dashboard) The LeMans on NW 2nd Avenue. Jean-Pierre's hand-drawn map unfolded on the steering wheel — pencil lines, precise notation, the Mesh-Hub marked with a tower-fish symbol. Axel's eyes reading it. The headlights off.
- **Panel 02:** (Wide/Alley) The alley behind the Mesh-Hub. Jean-Pierre's step-van filling the frame — copper wire canopy, amber glow at the seams. Two men with a flatbed cart loading equipment. The LeMans approaching from the alley entrance, dark.

**Page 2 (The Archive)**
- **Panel 03:** (Trunk/Close) The LeMans trunk open. Jean-Pierre loading: two receivers, the reel-to-reel, canvas tool roll. The wooden box going in last — plain, unfinished, held carefully. His hands on it: the way you hold something that cost everything.
- **Panel 04:** (Interior/Passenger) Jean-Pierre in the passenger seat, second map on the dashboard — updated tonight, ink wet on two corrections. Both maps visible: his February version on the wheel, tonight's version on the dash. Axel's hands at ten and two.

**Page 3 (The Route)**
- **Panel 05:** (Wide/Street) Little Haiti at 11 PM, no headlights. The street watching the LeMans move through it. Porch lights off, windows dark — not abandoned, inhabited and quiet. The fire glow from Liberty City visible to the west, low and orange. The car moving through darkness that has people in it.
- **Panel 06:** (Mid-shot/Porch) Jean-Pierre standing at an old man's porch, speaking in Creole, rapid and low. The old man pointing north twice. Axel visible in the car behind, waiting. Three minutes, no more.

**Page 4 (The Arrival)**
- **Panel 07:** (Wide/Palmetto) The LeMans on the Palmetto heading southwest, headlights back on. Behind them through the rear window: the Liberty City glow. The scanner on the dash active. Jean-Pierre watching it without looking directly at it.
- **Panel 08:** (Exterior/Garage) VÁLDES AUTO, roll-up door half open, work lights inside. The LeMans pulling up. Elio emerging from under the Cutlass — wiping hands, reading the car, reading the passenger, reading the trunk sitting lower than usual.
- **Panel 09:** (Interior/Garage) Jean-Pierre and Elio facing each other. Jean-Pierre: canvas bag, wooden box in arms. Elio: shop rag, expression of a man doing immediate triage on a situation. Ricky asleep in the folding chair, homework on the clipboard, completely undisturbed.

**Visual Generation Notes:**
- The absence of headlights is the visual key for Panels 01–07. The city should feel navigated by memory and instinct, not illumination
- The fire glow from Liberty City should be consistent — low, orange, to the west — in every exterior panel. It is always there. It is the context for everything.
- Jean-Pierre's wooden box: plain, nothing special-looking. Its value is not visible. That's the point.
- Panel 06: the porch conversation should feel warm and trusted despite the circumstances — these are people who know each other
- Ricky asleep in the folding chair in Panel 09 is important. The garage absorbs emergencies without drama.
