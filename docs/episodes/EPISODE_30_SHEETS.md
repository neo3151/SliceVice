# EPISODE 30 PANEL BREAKDOWN: "THE LEDGER RUN" (12 PANELS)
**Date:** May 18, 1980 — 2 AM to 4 AM
**Tone:** A garage holding the line. A drive north through a city that belongs to fire. Two men in suits meeting a retired man with a revolver and running out of arguments.
**Color Key:** Garage: single caged work-light amber. I-95 northbound: dark, high-beams cutting empty highway, fire glow in the rearview. The Fountainview hallway: fluorescent, institutional white. Sol's apartment: warm lamp light, lived-in. The .38: dull steel, nothing dramatic. The suits: crisp, corporate grey. The burning Miami through the 18th floor window: orange horizon, wide.

---

**Page 1 (The Garage)**
- **Panel 01:** (Interior/Garage) Elio's garage, 2 AM. Roll-up door down and deadbolted. Jean-Pierre on a milk crate with the Mesh archive reel. Elio standing with shop rag, skeptical. The single work-light overhead. The city muffled behind the corrugated metal — sirens, distant, low.
- **Panel 02:** (Mid-shot/Workbench) Jean-Pierre unspooling magnetic tape from the wooden box. Elio watching with the expression of a man who fixes things and has no use for things he cannot fix.
- **Panel 03:** (Close-up/Dashboard) Axel leaning against the LeMans hood, Nixie timer glowing steady green. Exhausted but wired. The light from the timer reflected in his eyes.

**Page 2 (The Drive)**
- **Panel 04:** (Wide/Highway) The LeMans north on an empty I-95. High-beams on. In the rearview mirror: the smoke column over Liberty City, oily black against the night sky. The highway completely empty. The car alone on it.
- **Panel 05:** (Interior/Speedometer) Looking over the steering wheel — speedometer at 85. Dashboard quiet. The transmission running smooth. The road ahead dark and straight.

**Page 3 (The Fountainview)**
- **Panel 06:** (Wide/Exterior) The Fountainview, Hollywood. Brutalist concrete tower. Two matte-black Vanguard sedans parked on the front lawn — not tactical vehicles, company cars. Understated. That's the point.
- **Panel 07:** (Hallway/Wide) The 18th floor hallway. Steady fluorescent light. Sol's door open — not breached, simply open. Two men in corporate grey suits visible just inside the doorway, clipboards, uncomfortable expressions. Axel stepping off the elevator behind them.

**Page 4 (The Room)**
- **Panel 08:** (Interior/Armchair) Sol in his armchair. The .38 on his lap — resting there, not raised, not pointed. His hands folded over it loosely. His expression: patient, waiting for someone to make a decision. The suits standing near the entrance looking at the revolver.
- **Panel 09:** (Wide/Doorway) Axel in the doorway, blocking it. Not aggressive — just present. Large, still, not moving. The suits between Sol in the armchair and Axel in the door. No good options visible to them.
- **Panel 10:** (Close-up/.38) The hammer being thumbed back. Sol's hand, the revolver, the mechanical click implied by the image. No drama — just the sound of a decision being made clear.

**Page 5 (The Exit)**
- **Panel 11:** (Wide/Window) Sol packing his leather suitcase. Through the 18th floor panoramic window behind him: Miami on the southern horizon, orange glow of the fires wide and low. The suits backing toward the hallway. Axel holding the door.
- **Panel 12:** (Wide/Exterior) The LeMans leaving the Fountainview, Sol in the passenger seat, suitcase on his lap. The Vanguard sedans remaining, useless. The car heading south toward the glow.

**Visual Generation Notes:**
- The Vanguard men are suits, not soldiers — clipboards, not weapons. They should look like bank auditors, which is what they are. Uncomfortable, not threatening.
- Sol's .38 is never raised or pointed in any panel. It sits on his lap. Its presence does all the work.
- The fire glow through the 18th floor window in Panel 11 should be wide and panoramic — the scale of what's happening, seen from above and far away
- Panel 09: Axel blocking the doorway is purely positional. No weapon, no aggression. Just a large man standing in the only exit.
- The Vanguard sedans in Panel 06: company cars, not armored vehicles. The institutional threat arrives quietly.
