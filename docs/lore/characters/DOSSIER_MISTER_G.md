# NEIGHBORHOOD DOSSIER: Mister G
## 👤 PROFILE / PURPOSE
Mister G is the unofficial "landlord" of the abandoned Art Deco laundromat beneath Axel’s rooftop shipping container in Sector H (Little Haiti). He is an elderly Haitian man who lives entirely inside the hollowed-out aluminum shell of three massive, industrial-grade commercial clothes dryers linked together along the back wall. The glass doors of the dryers serve as his porthole windows to the flooded street. He rarely speaks, communicating mostly through aggressive broom-sweeps and tapping out rhythms on the washing machines with a heavy iron wrench.

## 💲 THE SURVIVAL ECONOMY
The Vanguard grid shut off municipal water to Sector H two years ago, forcing residents to rely on contaminated rain runoff. However, the 1960s Art Deco laundromat was built directly over a forgotten, pre-corporate subterranean aquifer line that the Vanguard thermal-mapping missed. Mister G managed to hand-pump this deep, clean artisinal well water up through the building's ancient industrial pipes. He runs an illicit hydration market. In a city surrounded by blackwater and toxic rain, Mister G is the only source of uncontaminated drinking water for four blocks.

## 🤝 AXEL'S ANCHOR
There is a silent, almost hostile respect between the two. Axel’s presence on the roof is loud; the massive Pontiac LeMans draws attention when he jumps the curb to park it under the tarp. But Axel also brings heavy industrial power. They have a strict barter system: Axel provides him with half-dead, scavenged car batteries from his runs. In exchange, Mister G leaves three five-gallon jugs of aggressively cold, crystal-clear water at the rusted fire-escape ladder for Axel every Tuesday.

## 🗣️ THE VIGNETTE
The humidity inside the laundromat was overwhelming, smelling of ancient soap scum and wet concrete. Axel descended the rattling iron fire-escape, a heavy Vanguard-issue marine battery slung over his aching shoulder. The street outside was ankle-deep in blackwater, mirroring the flickering pink neon of a broken bodega sign. Axel dropped the battery onto a cracked linoleum tile near the front of the store with a loud thud. 

For a long moment, the dark room was completely silent. Then, one of the massive dryer doors at the back of the room swung open with a screech of rusted hinges. Mister G poked his head out, his face illuminated only by the soft orange glow of a battery-powered lantern inside his dryer-shell. He stared at the battery, then stared at Axel. Without a word, the old man rapped his iron wrench twice against the side of the machine. The hollow *clank-clank* echoed through the cavernous room. 

Axel didn't nod, he just stepped back into the shadows. Sitting next to the front door were three heavy plastic jugs, condensation weeping down their sides in the humid air. Axel scooped up two of the heavy jugs by their handles and made the long, slow climb back up the rusted ladder to the Tin Can. It was a good trade; the marine battery was totally dead, but Mister G wouldn't know that until tomorrow.
