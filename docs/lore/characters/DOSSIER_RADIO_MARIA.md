# NEIGHBORHOOD DOSSIER: "Radio" Maria 
## 👤 PROFILE / PURPOSE
"Radio" Maria is the nerve center of Sector H's Analog Sub-Mesh. She operates entirely out of the gutted back half of a rusted 1974 Chevrolet step-van (formerly a mail truck) parked permanently in the overgrown alley behind Axel's building. The van's roof bristles with an absurd, chaotic canopy of wire hangers, copper coils, and stolen CB-radio antennas. Maria is legally blind, wearing thick, heavily scratched aviator sunglasses at all hours. She navigates the interior of the cramped van entirely by touch, her fingers constantly dancing over a massive mixing board of ham radios, cassette decks, and police scanners.

## 💲 THE SURVIVAL ECONOMY
Maria doesn't trade in goods; she trades in Vanguard Blindspots. Her massive antenna array picks up the low-frequency hum of Vanguard surveillance drones and corporate "Audit Mortars" before they enter the sector. Because she is blind, her hearing is hyper-tuned to the specific audio-interference that corporate tracking lasers cause when they bounce off analog radio waves. She broadcasts localized warnings, safe-routes, and drone-schedules over a short-wave pirate frequency in rapid-fire Creole and English. The neighborhood feeds her, bringing her hot plates of griot, plantains, and scavenged engine parts to keep her van's alternator running. If Maria's radio goes silent, Sector H knows they are about to be raided.

## 🤝 AXEL'S ANCHOR
Axel acts as Maria's "Hardware Hound." Her pirate operation requires an obscene amount of analog replacement parts—vacuum tubes, specific gauge copper wire, and rare fuses that Vanguard outlawed in 1978. Axel's delivery routes across the city allow him to scavenge corporate junkyards in Sector B and the flooded Redlands. He drops the hardware through the rusted mail-slot of her van. In return, Maria provides Axel with highly customized, printed "Static Maps"—weather reports that predict exactly when and where the 3:00 PM flash floods will knock out Vanguard traffic cameras.

## 🗣️ THE VIGNETTE
The 3:00 PM monsoon had just broken, turning the alley into a shimmering mirror of rain and oil-slicks. Axel jogged the three blocks from his laundromat, his denim jacket pulled tight over his head. Under his arm, wrapped tightly in oilcloth, was a pristine CRT oscilloscope tube he’d ripped out of an abandoned corporate ferry. He approached the rusted step-van, the heavy bass of static and disjointed radio chatter vibrating the vehicle's thin metal frame. 

Axel didn't knock. He simply fed the heavy oilcloth package through the large mail-drop slot in the rear door. It hit the metal floor inside with a heavy *thump*. Almost instantly, a small, heavily scarred hand reached out through the slot, blindly feeling the air until it brushed against Axel's wet sleeve. The hand shoved a violently crumpled, yellowed index card and a single loose cigarette into his palm.

"They shifted the patrol on Biscayne," Maria's voice rasped from inside the dark van, competing with the hum of the radios. "Director Vance installed thermal-cams on the toll-booths at 8th street. You drive that route tonight, the LeMans is gonna light up like a Roman candle." Axel looked down at the index card, which had a crude map of an alternate route through a flooded golf course scrawled in blue crayon. He tucked the cigarette behind his ear, slapped the side of the van twice in thanks, and jogged back into the rain.
