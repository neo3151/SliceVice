# AXEL'S OFF-GRID AUDIO LOG: THE GHOST MONTH

**Date: March 17, 1980** | **Location: NW 36th Street, Allapattah**
* **Observation:** Fired up the LeMans at 0400. The humidity was already sitting at eighty percent, making the analog copper wire on the dashboard sweat. I burned through twenty Re-Enfranchisement Credits just to keep the Nixie Timer out of the red while idling at a red light on NW 36th. Across the street, an empty warehouse district. But the gravity was wrong. A localized Grievance Point heavy enough to make my ears pop. Two unmarked Vanguard F-350s rolled out of the bay doors, riding suspiciously low, like they were carrying solid steel. They headed east toward the water. 

**Date: March 21, 1980** | **Location: Versailles Restaurant, Calle Ocho, Little Havana**
* **Observation:** Picked up a dead-drop from Doña Carmen. She handed me a guava pastelito through the ventanita. Inside the wax paper: three scavenged vacuum tubes and eighty bucks in physical, untrackable cash. She didn't charge me for the coffee. "Eat," she said, her eyes tracking a city utility truck creeping past the cafe. Except it wasn't a utility truck. The thermal shielding under the chassis belonged to Vanguard. They are drawing maps of the analog dead zones. 

**Date: March 24, 1980** | **Location: Rickenbacker Causeway, Key Biscayne**
* **Observation:** Ran an envelope of encrypted flash-drives out to the old Marine Stadium. The ocean smelled like sulfur and impending rain. On the drive back, the Nixie Timer spiked yellow. I pulled over onto the shoulder. Out on the dark water of the straits, past the breakwater, Coast Guard cutters were running completely dark, searching for something. The static on my AM radio began to bleed into an organized, mechanical hum. The sound of Vance's extraction nets powering up somewhere in the deep water. 

**Date: March 28, 1980** | **Location: Dockery Salvage, Palm Springs Mile, Hialeah**
* **Observation:** Spent the morning ripping a heavy-duty alternator out of a wrecked '75 Impala. The Florida sun was brutal, baking the rusted metal until it burned to touch. Old man Hank Dockery was terrified. He said municipal trucks came in the night and forcefully confiscated five tons of his best scrap steel without paperwork, claiming "Metrorail eminent domain." They aren't building a train. They are building armor. I threw the alternator in the trunk and drove away fast, the LeMans grinding in second gear.

**Date: April 2, 1980** | **Location: Sector H (Little Haiti) / NE 54th Street**
* **Observation:** Dropped the alternator off for Jean-Pierre behind the Notre Dame d'Haiti church. He looked exhausted, soldering copper wire onto heavy circuit boards by the light of a single kerosene lamp. He's building something massive. He told me that the ambient thermal ping sweeps from Vanguard are tripling in frequency. "They are looking at the shoreline," he said, handing me a modified spark plug wrapped in foil. "They know something is coming from the south. Keep the engine running hot, Axel. The air is going to get very thin."

**Date: April 6, 1980** | **Location: Imperial Towers, Hollywood Beach**
* **Observation:** Drove up the Condo Canyon to grab a sandwich at the deli. Sol Abramowitz was in his usual booth, complaining that the city construction crews were working past midnight, keeping him awake. He thinks it's legitimate condo development. I looked out the diner window at the beach. Those weren't construction bulldozers. They were Vanguard terrestrial terraformers, carving deep, unnatural trenches into the sand. They are building physical funnels. Chokepoints.

**Date: April 10, 1980** | **Location: Underneath I-95 South, Overtown**
* **Observation:** Slept in the back seat of the LeMans with the engine barely turning over, just enough to feed the Nixie Timer the vacuum it needed to let me breathe. The tape reel in my jacket pocket felt heavy. The Blood Debt. The phantom hum of Vanguard servers trying to sync with my mother's consciousness. A Repo-Truck drove directly over my position on the overpass above. The Grievance Gravity hit so hard the LeMans’ suspension physically bottomed out against the dirt. 

**Date: April 14, 1980** | **Location: The Drowned District / SE 1st Avenue**
* **Observation:** Midnight. The rain started falling sideways. I parked the car under a corrugated tin awning near the docks. The dashboard scanner finally broke its silence. The encrypted Coast Guard bands were screaming. Unfiltered marine engines. Hundreds of them. The Vanguard floodlights down the beach suddenly ignited, turning the torrential rain into a blinding wall of white light. The ghost month is over. The nets are open.
