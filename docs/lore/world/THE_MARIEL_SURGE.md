# THE MARIEL SURGE: THE DATA FLOOD

**Historical Anchor: April - October 1980**
**Vanguard Threat Level:** Severe (Systemic Logistics Failure)

In the spring of 1980, the Vanguard's "Total Compliance" mainframe faced its first existential threat—not from armed rebel syndicates or Cartel wedge-cars, but from the arrival of 125,000 undocumented humans. 

When the Port of Mariel in Cuba was opened, a massive flotilla of "Cigarette Boats," fishing trawlers, and scavenged yachts crossed the Florida Straits, carrying refugees into a city totally unprepared to catalog them. The Vanguard, whose entire operational mandate is built on assigning "Existence IDs" to every metric asset in Dade County, experienced a catastrophic "Buffer Overrun."

---

## 1. THE DATA FLOOD & THE MAINFRAME FREEZE
Every time a refugee stepped onto the Florida coast, the Vanguard sensors struggled to generate a corporate debt-profile for them. 

- **The Overflow:** With thousands arriving daily, the central processing towers in the Neon Ziggurat overheated. To prevent total server meltdowns, the Vanguard suspended the processing of low-tier tracking data.
- **The Shadow Window:** Because the Vanguard had to divert 80% of its processing power to issuing temporary "Marielito Polled Targets," existing low-level anomalies (like Axel's '78 LeMans) fell completely off the Grid. For the first time since he inherited the tape, Axel could drive down Biscayne Boulevard without pinging a corporate toll.
- **The "Burn-Bag" Identifiers:** Refugees were given physical paper tags—"Burn Bags"—instead of digital implants or magnetic cards. These were highly counterfeit-able, leading to an immediate explosion of identity black-markets heavily controlled by the Cartels.

---

## 2. THE STRAITS BLOCKADE
Attempting to control the influx, Director Vance ordered the Vanguard to deploy their elite maritime units alongside corporate-subsidized Coast Guard cutters.

- **The Barricade:** A miles-long net of sonar buoy-jammers and heavily armed patrol interceptors attempted to lock down the Rickenbacker and the Port of Miami.
- **The Smugglers:** Local scavengers and Miami's infamous "Cigarette Boat" runners realized that punching a hole through the Vanguard barricade wasn't just humanitarian—it was highly profitable. Unregistered speedboats equipped with quad-outboard V8s ripped through the blockade, their wakes disrupting the sonar nets long enough for dozens of refugee rafts to slip past.

---

## 3. ELIO, DOÑA CARMEN, AND THE VENTANITA PIPELINE
The established Cuban-exile community in Little Havana saw the Vanguard’s treatment of the "Marielitos" as a profound betrayal.

- **Elio Valdés:** Axel's new mechanic, Elio, repurposed his garage from building transmission upgrades to converting heavy-duty Cartel trucks into transport rigs capable of moving dozens of refugees past Vanguard checkpoints undetected.
- **Doña Carmen's Intelligence Hub:** The *ventanita* (coffee window) became the premier intelligence exchange point in the city. Doña Carmen uses the rhythm of the *cafetera* steam and the passing of tiny *colada* cups to coordinate rescue operations right under the noses of the Vanguard.

---

## 4. AXEL'S OPPORTUNITY
While Director Vance is completely consumed by the massive systemic hemorrhage caused by the boatlift, Axel uses the chaos to aggressively upgrade the LeMans. The "Data Flood" masks the heavy electromagnetic interference radiating from his mother's tape inside the trunk's Safe. He is free to maneuver—but the streets are more dangerous than ever as Cartels and Vanguard forces clash over the new chaos.
